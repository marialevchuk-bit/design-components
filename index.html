01 Read this first

Five decisions shape everything below. If you only remember these, the rest follows.

  • 01The canvas is warm, not white. Every screen sits on ivory #FAF9F5 with cream #F0EEE6 panels. Pure white is reserved for raised cards and menus. Grey-white backgrounds read as "some other product."
  • 02One accent: clay. The coral #D97757 is the only brand colour. It carries primary actions, the send button, focus rings, links and the logo — and nothing competes with it. Everything else is warm neutral or muted semantic.
  • 03Claude speaks in serif, the UI speaks in sans. Assistant reading copy is set in the serif at a generous 162% line-height; buttons, labels and chrome are sans. This split is the single most recognisable thing about the interface.
  • 04There is a dark mode. A warm charcoal — never pure black. Every token has a light and a dark value; design both, and let surfaces stay warm in both.
  • 05Quiet by default, soft when it lifts. Most surfaces are flat with a hairline border. Only menus, dialogs and toasts cast a shadow, and even then it's soft and warm — never a hard drop.

02 Brand foundations

Claude is one brand, not a white label. The personality is warm, human and unhurried — an assistant that feels like a thoughtful colleague, not a dashboard. Three qualities drive every visual choice.

Warm

Earthy ivory and clay over cold blue-grey. The palette borrows from paper, book cloth and kraft — materials, not screens.

Calm

Generous whitespace, few borders, one accent. Nothing shouts. The reading experience is the interface.

Human

A serif voice, soft corners and gentle motion. Precise without feeling clinical.

The mark

Claude

The sunburst asterisk drawn here is a functional stand-in for layout and spacing — swap in the official trademarked artwork before anything ships. The mark appears in clay on ivory, or knocked out in on-clay on a clay tile. Wordmark is the serif at 600.

Design constraint

The accent is one colour. Resist adding a second brand hue for "variety" — kraft and manilla exist for illustration and decoration only, never for interactive elements. If two things both want the accent, one of them isn't primary.

04 Typography

Two families. A serif for display and for Claude's own reading copy; a grotesque sans for every piece of UI chrome. Specimens render in Fraunces and Hanken Grotesk as open stand-ins for the licensed faces.

The core split

Anything Claude "says" — answers, long-form reading — is serif. Anything the app "does" — buttons, menus, labels, inputs, metadata — is sans. Get this one rule right and the interface reads as Claude even before colour is applied.

Letter-spacing

Tracking is near-zero everywhere. Display and titles take -0.02em to -0.01em to tighten the serif; caption/eyebrow labels take +0.06em with uppercase. Body and code are always 0.

Note

ring is a focus halo, not a drop shadow — zero blur, 3px spread, clay at ~32%. Build it as a second stroke or box-shadow spread, not a blurred shadow. In dark mode shadows deepen (higher opacity) rather than lighten.

08 Components

Buttons

44px default height · 10px radius · 15px/500 label · sans. Primary is the only filled variant; everything else recedes. Hover, active and focus states all exist (unlike touch-only apps).

States — primary

Focus uses the clay ring on every variant. A destructive action is the danger fill — there is no separate destructive shape. Ghost is for tertiary actions that must not compete.

A design system stays consistent when everyone draws from the same tokens rather than re-deciding values. The names become a shared vocabulary — clay, surface, radius-16 — so a designer and an engineer are always talking about the same thing.

User messages are a cream bubble, right-aligned, sans, with one clipped corner. Claude's messages are full-width, avatar-led, and set in the serif — no bubble. The pulsing dots are the "thinking" state; a streaming answer reveals text with a soft fade.

09 Iconography

A single line style: 1.5–2px stroke, rounded caps and joins, drawn on a 24px grid. Icons are quiet and monoline — they never carry the accent unless they are the action.

PropertyValue
Grid24×24, drawn to a 20px live area
Stroke1.8px default (1.5 at 20px, 2 at 28px) · round caps & joins
Sizes16 · 20 · 24 · 28 — buttons use 16–18 internally
ColourcurrentColor — inherits text colour; clay only when the icon is the primary action
Rule

Icons stay monoline and inherit currentColor so they recolour correctly in dark mode and inside buttons for free. Don't hard-code a hex on an icon; let it take the ink of its context.

10 Motion

Motion is gentle and purposeful — it explains where things come from, it never performs. Three durations and one easing cover almost everything.

TokenDurationUsed for
motion-fast150msHovers, presses, toggles, focus rings
motion-base250msMenus, toasts, message fade-in
motion-slow400msDialogs, sheets, theme cross-fade
easecubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)The default curve — soft in, soft out

Signature moments: the streaming answer reveals tokens with a short fade, the thinking dots pulse at 1.2s, and the theme toggle cross-fades surfaces at 300ms. Everything respects prefers-reduced-motion — when set, transitions collapse to a simple opacity change.

One thing to get right

Model the theme as a variable mode, not two component sets. Every colour token resolves per mode, so a single component renders correctly in light and dark. Don't fork components per theme.
